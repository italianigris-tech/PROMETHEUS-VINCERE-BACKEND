# MAUL — Quality Doctrine and Build Plan

> Authoritative plan for MAUL short-form quality. This replaces the previous milestone history. Completed work remains in code and audit records; this document defines the next standard.
>
> MAUL is not a second editing universe. It reuses the strongest proven Joseph and landscape planning, typography, motion, sound, memory, and judgment capabilities through governed short-form adapters. Format-specific constraints may adapt shared intent; they may not silently discard it or rebuild a weaker parallel stack.

## Product outcome

MAUL turns one-principal-speaker talking-head or podcast footage into reviewable short-form edits competitive with strong human work. Success is not a valid vertical MP4. It requires source-faithful selection, art-directed typography, meaning-led pacing, clean framing, dialogue-first sound, purposeful licensed visuals, materially distinct treatments, rendered quality evidence, and authenticated human approval.

Optimization order: retention and clarity; conversion; creator-brand consistency; novelty. Factual integrity, intelligibility, rights, accessibility, isolation, and auditability are never traded for attention.

The visual target is the quality family represented by the governed Reference Corpus: editorial hierarchy, premium motion physics, meaningful negative space, typographic contrast, integrated imagery, and confident restraint. MAUL transfers abstract traits, never creator identity, exact compositions, unlicensed assets, or reference pixels. Source quality remains real: poor lighting, compression, focus, or coverage must be improved conservatively, covered legitimately, or disclosed—not hidden behind typography.

## S4 quality definition, scope, and oversight classes

`S4` is a user-facing aspiration, not an existing repository grade. MAUL maps S4 to the Golden Corpus `elite` tier and may display it only as a derived release state. S4 means that independent blind reviewers judge the edit inside the agreed cinematic reference family across all mandatory dimensions: editorial selection and structure; cut/pause/camera judgment; typography writing, pairing, layout, motion, and restraint; subject/evidence/background composition; color; dialogue/music/SFX; treatment coherence and originality; encoded stability; and source fidelity. One outstanding hard failure, one unverified mandatory dependency, or one source-limitation disclosure prevents the S4 label for that artifact.

`No errors whatsoever` is not a credible software promise. The enforceable S4 contract is zero known critical hard-gate failures in an accepted export, calibrated residual-risk evidence, authenticated human approval, and good-or-no-export behavior. A render may be technically correct and still fail S4; a source may be impossible to elevate to S4 without legitimate coverage and therefore return `no-approved-candidate`.

The implementation tracks oversight by timing and authority:

| Oversight class | Meaning | Current examples | Required action |
|---|---|---|---|
| blocking-now | omission can invalidate the architecture or quality claim before implementation | undefined S4 rubric; no Art Direction Plan; no Context Assembly Plan; no Shot Intent Matrix; no bounded revision loop; rich/runtime font-graph disconnect; statistically weak release claim | add contract and tests before implementation begins |
| contingent | outcome depends on a later measured implementation or available dependency | winning planner/critic model; matte quality and compute; eligible font binaries; asset coverage; source grade latitude | track now, resolve at its build stage, block/downgrade honestly |
| sequential | cannot be fully measured until the prior stage exists, but its gate must be specified now | perceptual calibration; held-out generalization; production drift; canary rollback; IRL/reward learning | define evidence and owner now, execute after prerequisite passes |
| out-of-scope | deliberately excluded from V1 rather than accidentally forgotten | multi-speaker panels, gameplay-first edits, music-video montage, fiction continuity, universal language/platform support | reject or route elsewhere; never imply universal support |

This classification is revisited at every stage exit. A newly discovered oversight receives a class, owner, prerequisite, failure effect, and evidence target; it is not hidden to preserve schedule.
## Quality reset: current truth

The first torture run proved lineage, timelines, treatment records, rendering, and thumbnails. It did not prove editing quality:

- Font Intelligence indexed 577 canonical descriptors, but MAUL rendered Arial.
- The corpus is not 577 export-safe fonts; rights, binaries, glyphs, weights, runtime loading, and role suitability remain eligibility gates.
- Treatment typography is prose, not resolved font IDs, assets, metrics, pairings, or load evidence.
- The repository already contains substantial Joseph and landscape typography/motion capability, but MAUL has no complete capability bridge to it.
- Catalog presence, preview behavior, and production-render support are not currently distinguished by one authoritative ledger.
- Dialogue captions and editorial title writing are not separate artifacts.
- Silence removal exists, but no semantic beat model governs rhythm.
- The same zoom restarts across source segments without regard to meaning or gesture.
- One fallback crop covers the whole short.
- B-roll, proof, diagram, and brand policies are not executable renderer inputs.
- The composition carries an audio-plan ID but does not execute its ducking events.
- A technical render can be labeled aesthetically sound without perceptual evidence.
- A scripted reviewer can imitate human approval.
- Treatments differ cosmetically rather than editorially.
- Render speed and deterministic audio replay missed the torture thresholds.

These are contract failures. Cosmetic improvement cannot solve them.

### Scope of the failed evidence

The failed short exercised the thin `MaulShort` composition, not the Joseph landscape production composition. It is therefore not evidence that Joseph or landscape lost their richer editing capacity, and it must not be recorded as a landscape regression. It is evidence that MAUL bypassed that capacity: the current short renderer does not consume the shared typography, animation, camera, visual, or executable audio contracts.

Joseph and landscape already have materially richer native primitives and orchestration than the failed short. “World-class,” however, remains a claim to prove with representative golden renders, cross-format invariance tests, and blind perceptual review. IRL learning is a major remaining constraint, but it is not the only one: shared-kernel integration, font and motion render certainty, preview/final parity, and rendered quality evidence are also open proof obligations.

## Accountability conclusion: what “apply Joseph to shorts” means

Joseph is the correct editorial foundation, but it is not one finished callable editor that can be resized from landscape to portrait. It is a body of encoded doctrine, planners, primitives, render branches, evidence rules, and some partially executed intentions. MAUL must turn that body of work into a deep Shared Creative Kernel with one small interface and two real adapters: the landscape/Joseph adapter and the portrait/short-form adapter. Copying Joseph heuristics into MAUL would create another shallow parallel stack and repeat the failure.

| Transfer class | What belongs here | Required behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Editorial invariants | source truth, rhetorical roles, Sequence Objective, Protected Pauses, Negative Grammar, motivated cuts, emphasis ownership, contrast, restraint, payoff, dialogue priority, repetition control | identical reason and meaning across landscape and portrait |
| Format adaptations | canvas geometry, crop, safe zones, reading distance, text occupancy, hook latency, bounded timing envelopes, gesture room, evidence placement, platform reserve | changed only through a versioned adapter decision with evidence |
| Portrait/short-form additions | aggressive crop recovery, face/hand occlusion, text-behind-subject compositing, mobile typographic scale, short-form fatigue, platform overlays, vertical B-roll/PiP, rapid candidate comparison | explicit new contracts; never inferred from a similarly named Joseph feature |

Joseph behavior is adopted only when its full path is proven: annotated principle or governed doctrine → planner decision → manifest field → native renderer behavior → encoded evidence → Judgment Layer result. Prose, a candidate record, a CSS name, a route label, or an unused manifest field is not transferred capability.

The reference images establish still-frame taste—hierarchy, type pairing, negative space, warmth, composition, restraint, and integration. They do not establish easing, dwell, cut timing, camera settlement, transition logic, matte continuity, or audio choreography. Motion parity therefore requires a governed motion-reference set with timestamped annotations in addition to supplied still references and Joseph landscape annotations.

No finite plan can guarantee cinematic quality for every source, language, asset condition, or unseen creative problem. The enforceable guarantee is stronger and more honest: **MAUL produces a reference-family candidate that passes rendered and human gates, or returns `no-approved-candidate`; it never exports a mediocre result under a cinematic-success label.** Poor source, missing rights, insufficient matte quality, unavailable coverage, or judge uncertainty can legitimately produce no export.

## First principles

1. Meaning precedes movement.
2. One moment has one dominant viewer focus.
3. Typography is writing, hierarchy, placement, duration, and motion—not a font name.
4. Captions preserve speech; editorial statements compress source-supported meaning.
5. Every cut needs an editorial cause. Silence is evidence, not an instruction.
6. Camera choreography is continuous across implementation segments.
7. Dialogue has absolute sonic priority.
8. Libraries are constrained search spaces, not rotation quotas.
9. References provide abstract traits, never export pixels or identity markers.
10. Models propose governed intent; deterministic contracts execute it.
11. A quality claim requires rendered evidence.
12. Uncertainty degrades safely or blocks export.
13. Shared capability is reused before new capability is invented.
14. Format adaptation changes constraints, not editorial meaning.
15. Capability does not imply use: the best edit may deliberately remain still.
16. Every selected animation declares its semantic role, granularity, timing, native render branch, and evidence.
17. “All animations” means every eligible render-safe capability is addressable—not that every capability appears in one video.
18. Joseph is transferred by preserved doctrine and observable behavior, not copied constants or matching names.
19. A configured model route is not a planner invocation; model work requires an auditable request, response, schema result, and effect on the selected plan.
20. A deterministic system can be reproducible and still have poor taste; determinism and cinematic judgment are separate proof obligations.
21. Frame-driven motion can be deterministically ugly; encoded temporal stability and perceptual quality must both pass.
22. Templates constrain craft and safety; they may not replace semantic planning or force identical scenes.
23. “Implemented” means native-rendered, encoded-output-verified, held-out-tested, and approved—not schema-complete or code-complete.
24. The release contract is good-or-no-export, not always-export and not zero-failure mythology.

Every artifact declares source, parents, version, replay key, warnings, and fallbacks.

### Authoritative Editorial Timeline

The factual time model: words, sentences, voice, silence, breaths, confidence, Protected Pauses, cut boundaries, audio handles, source/output mapping, shots, gestures, motion, speaker tracks, and technical warnings.

### Candidate Narrative

The coherent hook, orientation, escalation, proof, reveal, payoff, and source-supported CTA. Every factual statement points to source evidence. If the source cannot support a coherent result, return fewer candidates or none.

### Editorial Beat Map

The common cause behind rhythm. Each beat records time range, rhetorical role, spoken idea, intensity, information density, gesture/shot state, dominant focus, allowed cuts/camera/type/visual/audio events, continuity, rationale, and confidence.

### Planner Accountability Contract

The runtime bill of materials for every planning stage: module, deterministic or model authority, provider, exact model/version, endpoint class, prompt and schema fingerprints, available visual/audio/text context, context-window policy, temperature/seed, confidence calibration, retry/fallback policy, latency/cost, request receipt, response hash, and the plan fields changed by the result. A configured route or evidence label without an inference receipt is recorded as `not-invoked`.

### Doctrine Traceability Matrix

Every Joseph rule and supplied annotation receives a stable doctrine ID, source reference/time range, editorial claim, invariant/adaptable/portrait-only classification, planner rule, manifest field, renderer branch, Render Contract Test, encoded evidence pointer, and status. `Joseph parity` is blocked while a required row terminates in prose, metadata, an unused field, or a renderer that does not visibly execute it.

### Genome Composition Plan

The macro Treatment Genome, scene/beat subgenomes, inherited motifs, permitted crossover points, incompatible genes, transition/handoff rules, novelty budget, repetition budget, and intended recalls. It distinguishes diversity across candidate treatments from controlled intertwining inside one short. Every borrowed gene retains semantic reason and dialect compatibility; random mixing and whole-video single-skin templating are both failures.

### Typography and Motion Plan

Source-grounded editorial copy; caption groups; text roles; selected font IDs and files; family, weight, style, axes, fallback; pairing rationale; rights and load evidence; size, leading, tracking, case, alignment, safe region, line breaks, emphasis sources, measured dimensions, overflow, and glyph coverage.

Every motion event also declares source text range, semantic role, granularity (`block`, `line`, `word`, `glyph`, `stroke`, or `composite`), registry primitive/program ID, motion dialect, entry/hold/exit frames, easing, stagger, target token or grapheme, synchronized beat/audio event, composition dependencies, collision budget, accessibility constraints, native branch, fallback, and evidence pointer.

### Framing and Camera Plan

Time-varying subject bounds, crop, scale, headroom, eye line, gesture accommodation, tracking confidence, smoothing, velocity/acceleration/jerk limits, motivated reframes, continuity, docking state, and fallbacks.

### Subject/Depth Compositing Plan

A time-varying layer graph—background plate/video, behind-subject typography or graphics, subject foreground, front overlays—with matte/depth source, alpha format, premultiplication, edge refinement, hair/hand/finger handling, spill control, temporal confidence, occlusion ownership, performance tier, and fallback. If compute is unavailable, the desired z-order remains recorded as `accounted-deferred` with estimated cost, reason, and selected safe substitute; it is never silently flattened into ordinary front text.

### Background Scene Plan

Time-bound background events with beat reason, source/plate, crop, depth/parallax intent, camera match, lighting/white-balance match, contact/grounding treatment, defocus, continuity, transition, rights, and fallback. A static color token is not evidence of background-change capability.

### Motion Graphics Intent and Slot Plan

Optional semantic slots for diagrams, callouts, proof graphics, maps, data, interfaces, annotations, and decorative accents. Each slot records need, beat, query, asset availability, alpha/color space, anchor/track, z-layer/occlusion, entrance-hold-exit, brand adaptation, retiming, collision cost, fallback, and `needed-but-unavailable` evidence. Zero available motion assets must preserve intent and produce a restrained fallback, not erase the planner decision.

### Color Pipeline Plan

Input color metadata, normalization transform, per-shot matching, technical transform, creative look or LUT ID/hash/license, strength envelope, skin-tone protection, highlight/shadow limits, noise/banding policy, SDR/HDR output transform, scopes evidence, and fallback. “Warm,” “cinematic,” or a CSS filter is not an executable grade.

### Visual Plan

Approved speaker frames, B-roll, proof imagery, charts, diagrams, brand assets, scene mode, relevance, provenance, crop, duration, hierarchy, and fallback. Irrelevant, unsafe, or Reference Corpus pixels are excluded.

### Dialogue-First Audio Plan

Dialogue cleanup/gain; zero or more music tracks, stems, and sections; source/rights per track; trims, downbeats, fades, overlaps, bridges, BPM/key compatibility, crossfades, bus routing, and automation; speech ducking; SFX with beat reasons; loudness/peak targets; room tone and J/L-cut handles; continuity warnings; and deterministic mix inputs. Every scheduled audio event must reach an observable native audio node or an explicit fallback.

### Quality Evidence Bundle

Rendered contact sheets, caption measurements, crop coverage, cut probes, audio analysis, provenance, source-fidelity checks, treatment conformance, primitive conformance, reference-trait comparison, repetition, replay comparisons, performance, cost, integrity, and unresolved warnings.

### Thumbnail Plan

Three source-grounded candidates with contiguous-source copy, authentic source frames, rights/provenance, safe crop, brand treatment, approximately 180-pixel-wide legibility evidence, reference-pixel exclusion, and authenticated review. Thumbnail generation may recompose source pixels and type; it may not fabricate expressions, proof, claims, or creator identity.
### Art Direction Plan

The per-run cinematic thesis: audience and intent; emotional temperature; source-respect stance; theme; palette and color intent; type roles and pairings; layout/negative-space logic; imagery and background language; camera behavior; motion physics; annotation grammar; sound world; motif introduction/development/recall; treatment-specific variation; and explicit prohibitions. It translates creator/brand constraints and approved Reference Traits into one coherent direction before individual effects are selected. A Treatment Genome may develop the direction; it may not become an unrelated style pack.

### Context Assembly Plan

The multi-scale view supplied to the Top-Level Planner: whole-source synopsis and narrative phases; candidate-clip neighborhood; local transcript/prosody; shot/contact-sheet windows; face/gesture/motion windows; named facts, callbacks, setup/payoff dependencies, chronology, source-quality changes, and unresolved uncertainty. It declares what was omitted or summarized because of context limits. The Adaptive Planning Horizon handles local decisions, but it cannot erase whole-source context needed to understand the hook, consequence, or payoff.

### Shot Intent Matrix

Every output shot or continuous source interval records source/output range, editorial purpose, rhetorical role, selected content, in/out reason, continuity relationship, screen direction, pose/gesture state, eye line, crop/camera target, evidence/background/docking state, text opportunity, audio handles, color match, transition, confidence, and fallback. A planned cut must create two valid neighboring shot records; an implementation segment is not automatically a shot.

### Text Opportunity Plan

Every beat is evaluated for dialogue captions, editorial hero/support/proof text, annotation, utility, CTA, or deliberate absence. Each opportunity records communication benefit, source support, viewer reading load, available negative space, subject occlusion, speech rate, concurrent imagery, duration, hierarchy owner, and reason to use or withhold text. Dialogue captions may be frequent in short form; editorial typography remains selective. Text frequency is an outcome of comprehension and art direction, never a quota or proof of editing activity.

### Revision Plan

Failure-specific repair options, immutable fields, allowed Genome mutations, maximum attempts, time/cost budget, critic independence, escalation, human checkpoint, and stop reason for each candidate. It prevents one-shot optimism as well as endless self-revision.

## Shared Creative Kernel and format adapters

The Shared Creative Kernel is the reusable editing intelligence beneath Joseph, landscape, and MAUL. It owns common semantic and craft behavior; it is not a new competing orchestration stack.

It must reuse or consolidate:

- Observation Snapshot and Planning Snapshot facts;
- Candidate Narrative, Editorial Beat Map, Treatment Genome, Sequence Objective, and Negative Grammar;
- Sequence Memory for within-run contrast and Pattern Memory for reviewed reusable outcomes;
- typography intelligence, font compatibility, writing roles, animation registries, cinematic motion grammar, and text safety;
- scene choreography, asset retrieval, evidence/B-roll roles, camera reasoning, transition policy, and motion budgets;
- dialogue-first audio planning and sound-event motivation;
- Manifest Compiler, Render Contract Tests, Quality Evidence Bundle, Judgment Layer, and Review Surface.

Shared modules return format-neutral intent where possible. A format adapter may resolve canvas coordinates, safe zones, scale, reading duration, density, or timing envelopes. It may not replace a selected primitive with a generic effect, discard a Protected Pause, invent a new semantic emphasis, or mutate source truth.

### Short Cadence Adapter

The Short Cadence Adapter converts shared sequence intent into short-form timing. It is not a fixed speed multiplier and does not compress landscape timing blindly.

It considers hook latency, rhetorical role, speech rate, prosody, pause meaning, information density, reading time, gesture completion, shot motion, source resolution, visual complexity, audio energy, prior/future beats, platform reserve, and viewer fatigue. It may shorten development, bring evidence forward, or increase contrast only when comprehension and continuity remain sound.

The landscape and Joseph “homework” is therefore preserved: the same reasons govern selection, restraint, emphasis, repetition, and payoff; only the temporal and spatial priors change for short form.

### Portrait Format Adapter

The Portrait Format Adapter resolves the shared plan for a 9:16 canvas: subject-safe crop, headroom, gesture room, negative space, platform UI reserve, mobile reading distance, maximum text occupancy, B-roll crop, PiP geometry, and camera limits. Safe areas use versioned TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts profiles with UI-overlay previews and platform-specific evidence; “generic 9:16 safe” is not sufficient. It must prefer recomposition or a different scene mode over damaging the subject or shrinking text below legibility.

## Joseph doctrine transfer and the deep shared seam

The Shared Creative Kernel exposes one format-neutral planning interface: factual observations, source-supported narrative, Beat Map, production constraints, creator/brand constraints, and requested behavior dimensions enter; governed editorial intent, candidate Treatment Genomes, sequence reasons, dependencies, and confidence leave. Landscape/Joseph and portrait/MAUL are two adapters at this seam. This is a real seam because the adapters vary geometry and cadence while the kernel preserves meaning.

The kernel earns its depth only if deleting it would force Joseph and MAUL to reimplement sequence reasoning, typography roles, camera reasons, sound motivation, Negative Grammar, memory, and evidence policy. Pass-through wrappers do not qualify. Tests cross the same interface as production callers; private implementation structure is not the proof surface.

The Doctrine Traceability Matrix must cover, at minimum:

- hook, orientation, build, revelation, payoff, release, hold, breath, punch, detonation, and flow doctrines;
- motivated cut legality, false-start removal, semantic handles, J/L cuts, room tone, pose continuity, cutaway coverage, and Protected Pauses;
- camera anticipation, cut-plus-reframe choreography, punch timing, settlement, hold, recovery, and prohibition of per-segment reset zooms;
- text-role ownership, emphasis ownership, negative space, hierarchy development, deliberate stillness, and anti-repetition;
- evidence/B-roll cause, background-change cause, docking/PiP entry-dock-handoff-exit, and matte availability;
- dialogue priority, music-section development, cross-track transitions, SFX causality, silence, and climax budgeting.

For each doctrine, a cross-format golden test uses the same source moment. The reason, factual claim, rhetorical role, Protected Pause status, emphasis owner, and sequence consequence remain invariant. Landscape and portrait may differ only in adapter-governed coordinates, crop, safe zones, reading duration, density, and bounded timing. Any other difference requires an explicit reviewed editorial decision.

## Planner authority and multimodal cognition

Current truth is recorded before implementation: the live MAUL planner is deterministic timeline construction, a fixed treatment catalogue, and shallow arithmetic scoring; the live Joseph path is deterministic seeded search and governed heuristics. Neither path currently invokes Codex, Grok/xAI, Llama, or an OpenAI critic as its editorial planner. A configured `groq/llama-3.3-70b-versatile` route or configured critic string is metadata until an inference receipt proves otherwise. Groq and Grok are not interchangeable names.

The target is not a one-shot omniscient model. It is a governed ensemble:

1. deterministic Media Analysis produces immutable transcript, prosody, shot, face, gesture, motion, source-quality, color, and audio facts;
2. a pinned multimodal planning adapter interprets those facts, contact sheets, low-resolution temporal probes, approved reference traits, Joseph doctrine, and production constraints into bounded Doctrine Branches and Treatment Genomes;
3. deterministic schedulers resolve cuts, reading time, collision budgets, crop, layer order, audio events, and renderer-compatible frames;
4. specialized vision/matte/crop/color/audio analyzers verify execution prerequisites;
5. the Manifest Compiler produces the only renderer input;
6. a separately configured and calibrated perceptual critic evaluates encoded pixels and audible output without seeing candidate labels or generator self-scores;
7. authenticated human review decides release.

The planning adapter is replaceable only through the Planner Accountability Contract. A model is eligible only after evaluation on semantic fidelity, cut preference, hierarchy, treatment diversity, reference-trait reasoning, uncertainty calibration, schema validity, latency, cost, and adversarial prompt resistance. Text-only planning cannot claim visual composition, matte, crop, gesture, or motion judgment. A future/placeholder ocular route cannot satisfy the vision requirement.

Every run records which authority decided each field. Deterministic fallback may preserve a restrained edit, but it cannot inherit the confidence or capability label of an unavailable multimodal planner. The model may propose; it may not fabricate source facts, declare assets licensed, bypass hard gates, select unavailable renderer branches, approve its own output, or silently rewrite Joseph invariants.

### Capability and implementation labels

A capability moves through the following irreversible-evidence states:

1. `planned`: doctrine and intended contract exist;
2. `implemented-unverified`: implementation exists but has no native encoded proof;
3. `native-render-verified`: the intended branch produces its distinctive observable behavior;
4. `encoded-output-verified`: final encoded video/audio passes stability, parity, and delivery probes;
5. `held-out-reference-parity-passed`: diverse unseen sources beat the agreed baseline under blind review;
6. `human-approved`: an authenticated reviewer approved this artifact version.

`complete`, `cinematic`, `Joseph-parity`, `all-animations-supported`, and `reference-quality` are derived labels, never manually assigned. They require every mandatory dependency to reach the specified state. `blocked`, `accounted-deferred`, `governed-fallback`, and `no-approved-candidate` are successful accountability outcomes, not hidden failures. Code compilation, a route label, a schema object, a plan ID, or a green unit test cannot advance a capability beyond `implemented-unverified`.

## Global-to-local understanding and bounded render-critique-revise

Cinematic judgment uses two simultaneous timescales. The Context Assembly Plan preserves the whole-source narrative, chronology, recurring ideas, quality changes, and candidate neighborhood. The Adaptive Planning Horizon then makes high-resolution local decisions without losing setup/payoff or creating a hook that misrepresents what follows. Tests include late hooks, callbacks outside the clip, false chronology, repeated ideas, source-quality changes, and a locally exciting moment that is globally incoherent.

The system is not expected to achieve S4 in one generative pass. After deterministic preflight, it renders low-cost native probes and a candidate draft. The independent Judgment Layer emits named failures tied to pixels/audio and doctrine IDs. A bounded revision controller may then repair only the affected fields: reselect a cut, change layout, choose a compatible font pair, simplify motion, change coverage, improve ducking, reject a matte, adjust color, or mutate a permitted Genome gene.

Every revision preserves immutable source facts and lineage, records before/after manifest and evidence, predicts the intended improvement, and re-runs all affected gates. It cannot lower thresholds, swap the critic, erase a warning, exceed cost, oscillate between prior states, or accumulate unrelated embellishment. Maximum cycles and no-progress detection terminate in human review, governed fallback, or `no-approved-candidate`. The released result is the best approved candidate after bounded repair, not automatically the first render.
## Treatment Genome composition and template grammar

A candidate has one macro Treatment Genome for its editorial promise and a sequence of scene/beat subgenomes. Intertwining is controlled development, not random style mixing. The planner may borrow a compatible gene when a rhetorical role changes, evidence arrives, a motif needs recall, or contrast is earned. It must declare donor genome, inherited trait, crossover beat, transition grammar, duration, semantic reason, compatibility proof, novelty cost, and return/recall behavior.

Hard rules:

- one macro identity remains recognizable across the short;
- each scene normally has one dominant dialect and one emphasis owner;
- a subgenome may change typography, camera, evidence, background, motion, or sound grammar only when the Beat Map changes role;
- inherited motifs develop or resolve; they do not recur as arbitrary decoration;
- incompatible easing, font, density, camera, layer, or sound genes cannot coexist without a designed handoff;
- the Sequence Objective penalizes both whole-video sameness and incoherent mutation;
- Sequence Memory prevents adjacent repetition; Pattern Memory influences selection only after reviewed evidence;
- three candidate treatments must differ in narrative emphasis, rhythm, hierarchy, camera, visuals, and audio; not merely in gene names.

Templates are bounded craft grammars. A template may define safe regions, typographic ratios, layer slots, docking states, motion envelopes, and fallbacks. It cannot choose the message, force a cut, assign emphasis, select a font without eligibility, repeat one entrance across every shot, or override subject geometry. The planner fills template slots from the Beat Map; the adapter resolves them; the renderer executes them. Template IDs and parameter differences are exposed in the Planner Audit so repeated-template fatigue can be measured.

## Encoded temporal-stability and no-jitter contract

Frame-driven Remotion motion is necessary but not sufficient. Every text, camera, matte, background, docking, and motion-graphics path is tested after final encoding for deterministic ugliness as well as nondeterminism.

The evidence suite measures:

- glyph and line bounding-box continuity, baseline drift, unexpected line reflow, transform-origin changes, and stable line-break IDs;
- subpixel shimmer, edge ringing, mask chatter, matte flicker, alpha/premultiplication halos, hair/hand discontinuity, and foreground-background edge crawl;
- position, scale, rotation, blur, opacity, tracking, crop, and camera velocity/acceleration/jerk across entry, hold, mutation, cut, handoff, and exit;
- static-region pixel stability so unrelated source motion cannot satisfy or hide a conformance test;
- preview/final and pre-encode/post-encode parity at representative frames and temporal windows;
- variable-font axis stability, loaded-font metric identity, glyph ownership, grapheme integrity, and bidirectional ordering;
- transition duration math, source/output frame mapping, and absence of one-frame flashes, blank frames, accidental resets, or late layers.

Fixed-layout wrappers reserve final dimensions before motion starts. Typewriter programs reveal by grapheme-safe string slicing; true glyph programs use explicit grapheme nodes. Motion timing is normalized once and derived into related properties so elements that belong together cannot drift. Thresholds are calibrated against human-labelled stable and unstable examples; exceeding a threshold blocks or chooses the declared lower-motion fallback. A restrained fallback must pass the same perceptual and reference-trait floor.
## Animation Capability Ledger

One generated, versioned Animation Capability Ledger is authoritative for what MAUL may select. It covers repository primitives, composites, SVG typography programs, cinematic treatments, caption modes, Joseph micro-animations, motion assets, and future additions.

Each entry records:

- stable ID, version, source module, owner, semantic roles, supported formats, and renderer host;
- granularity: block, line, word, glyph, stroke, or composite;
- status: `native-render-safe`, `adaptable`, `preview-only`, `prototype-only`, `prohibited`, `deprecated`, or `missing`;
- native render branch and observable output;
- compatible/incompatible primitives, layer order, concurrency limit, and repetition cost;
- timing envelope, easing/stagger limits, minimum readable hold, and exit policy;
- font, glyph, SVG, mask, matte, WebGL, asset, and audio dependencies;
- mobile safe-zone, overflow, contrast, flash, motion-sensitivity, and performance limits;
- deterministic fallback, fallback cost, failure tags, test evidence, and rendered proof.

Eligibility is fail-closed. A preview-only HTML/CSS prototype, a Tailwind animation class, an unverified generated asset, or a catalog record without a frame-driven production branch cannot be selected for export.

The current repository baseline to preserve and expose includes:

- twelve implemented SVG typography programs, including character stagger/drop/fog, script-plus-bold hierarchies, split impact, dual wipe, elastic hierarchy, and cursor/typed reveal;
- fifteen native Joseph micro-animation branches covering word and letter entry, tracking, velocity and mask reveals, underline/highlight/capsule/marker/glow emphasis, weight/handoff mutation, bracket/rail accents, and anchored drift;
- long-form word-by-word typography and emphasis adornments;
- cinematic split, blur, focus, rotation, mask, directional wipe, and pulse grammar;
- typewriter, blur reveal, highlight, circle, underline, replaceable-word composite, and their governed layer relationships.

This list mixes proven native paths with planned/prototype entries. In particular, an HTML prototype or a registry item whose implementation status is `planned` is not counted as native capacity until the ledger points to a production renderer and evidence. Baseline inclusion means “must be classified and either bridged or explicitly quarantined,” not “already export-ready.”

Counts are regression baselines, not a closed list. New capability appears in MAUL only after ledger generation, classification, native render evidence, and policy tests.

Discovery is zero-orphan: every exported registry entry, renderer component, SVG program, HTML/CSS prototype, motion asset, and alias is discovered and classified. The ledger reports counts and deltas by status in CI, records duplicate/alias relationships, and fails when an item is unclassified or disappears without a versioned migration. “Supported by the index” means discoverable metadata only; it never implies production-render support.

### Native render contract

All production motion is derived from `useCurrentFrame()`, explicit frame ranges, `interpolate()`, approved easing, and deterministic inputs. CSS transitions, CSS keyframes, and Tailwind animation classes are prohibited for export-authoritative motion. Static CSS styling remains allowed. Preview/control-surface transitions may exist when they are explicitly classified `preview-only`, but they cannot serve as export evidence or receive final approval.

Typewriter effects reveal text by string/grapheme slicing rather than independent opacity flicker. True glyph animation uses grapheme-aware nodes and explicit per-glyph transforms; it must not split surrogate pairs, combining marks, emoji sequences, ligatures, or bidirectional text incorrectly.

Fonts load before measurement. Measurement and rendering use identical family, real weight, style, axes, size, tracking, and line-height values, and font-loaded validation is enabled. Every event has a readable hold. Entry, hold, mutation, and exit phases remain separately inspectable.

The renderer must either:

1. execute the selected native branch observably;
2. execute the declared governed fallback and record the deviation; or
3. block the treatment.

Silent primitive collapse is a hard failure.

## Font corpus and compatibility-graph accountability

The inventory count is generated from the canonical discovery snapshot; no spoken or hard-coded count is authoritative. Current repository evidence reports 577 canonical descriptors, not 507, but the plan must survive additions, removals, aliases, and quarantines. The ledger records discovered descriptors, unique binaries, aliases, missing binaries, rights status, eligible role/language coverage, and exact deltas per version.

Two graphs currently exist and must not be conflated: the rich 577-node intelligence graph is materially broader but historically unwired, while the small runtime graph is connected to selection but narrow and biased. The target is one governed runtime resolution path that consumes eligible nodes and edges from the discovered corpus, with a curated doctrine overlay rather than a separate placeholder universe.

Graph use is causal and testable:

- single-font role ranking and multi-font pairing are separate decisions;
- pair selection traverses compatible/support/contrast/fallback edges and blocks avoid/forbidden/redundant combinations where policy requires;
- role, language, glyph, rights, binary, real weight/style/axis, motion tolerance, phone legibility, treatment, Art Direction Plan, recent-use penalty, and renderer availability filter before taste ranking;
- the Planner Audit records considered nodes/edges, rejected reasons, selected edge path, graph version, fallback path, and counterfactual winner without the graph;
- counterfactual tests prove that changing an edge changes the pair, removing a binary removes its node, a disconnected role falls back visibly, and forbidden pairs cannot win through a high aesthetic score;
- pairing contact sheets and encoded motion specimens prove that graph-compatible does not merely mean metadata-compatible;
- no rotation quota forces corpus utilization; eligible variety is measured across a suite, while each individual edit uses only the fonts its hierarchy needs.

`All fonts utilized` therefore means every discovered descriptor has a disposition and every eligible font can enter the same governed resolver. It does not mean all fonts appear, all 577 are licensed/renderable, or variety outranks suitability.
## Motion dialects

A motion dialect is a constrained grammar, not a preset skin. It controls eligible primitives, easing, entry velocity, overshoot, blur, stagger, tracking, scale, depth, hold, exit, density, camera coordination, and sound punctuation.

Required initial dialects:

- **Apple Editorial:** crisp deceleration, mask/blur resolution, synchronized layout movement, restrained scale/depth, near-zero overshoot, generous holds, clean negative space, and quiet exits;
- **Cinematic Serif:** high-contrast serif hierarchy, script/italic accent, slower reveals, warm composition, deliberate tracking, and confident stillness;
- **Kinetic Creator:** word/glyph staging, stronger contrast, selective elastic or impact motion, fast comprehension, and strict fatigue control;
- **Documentary Authority:** source/proof priority, sober serif/sans hierarchy, maps/quotes/labels when evidenced, restrained camera, and minimal SFX;
- **Minimal Founder:** neutral captions, sparse editorial statements, subtle camera, Protected Pauses, and silence as authority;
- **Bold Graphic Direct Response:** benefit/proof/payoff hierarchy, controlled graphic contrast, counters only when sourced, faster beat development, and no fake urgency.

Dialect selection is governed by treatment, rhetorical role, creator brand, source conditions, recent Sequence Memory, and reference traits. It cannot make an ineligible capability eligible. A scene normally uses one entry family, one emphasis family, and one exit family; exceptions need Beat Map evidence and must stay within the visual-chaos cap.

## Typography and writing policy

The reference-derived target is an original serif-led editorial language: high-contrast display serif, stable neutral/condensed sans, rare italic or script accent, asymmetric negative space, strong scale contrast, syntactic line breaks, warm controlled imagery, meaningful motion, and confident stillness. Named creator identity, exact layouts, signature combinations, trademarked language, unlicensed fonts, and reference pixels are non-transferable.

Text roles:

- dialogue caption: faithful, fast, restrained;
- hero statement: two to eight source-grounded words;
- proof statement: exact evidence with a source pointer;
- editorial support: qualifier, mechanism, context, attribution;
- utility label: episode/source/stage information;
- Governed CTA: approved facts and destination after proof.

The transcript remains immutable. Derived writing may remove filler without changing meaning. It may not invent claims, numbers, outcomes, offers, destinations, endorsements, urgency, or causality. Quotes require contiguous speech. Low-confidence words cannot become hero/proof copy without review. Line breaks follow syntax; negations, names, units, and essential word pairs may not be stranded.

Font roles are restricted to Hero Serif Primary, Hero Serif Alternate, Editorial Serif Support, Neutral Sans Core, Display Sans Pressure Release, and rare Script/Italic Accent. Ordinary scenes use at most two families; a short uses at most three.

Font eligibility precedes taste: commercial rights; real non-corrupt binary; deterministic hash; required real weight/style; language, punctuation, numeral, symbol, currency, and diacritic coverage; phone readability; runtime load; and non-review-only status. Personal-use/demo/trial/unknown fonts, faux weights, and silent Arial fallback are blocked.

Eligible fonts rank by role, Treatment Genome, readability/expressiveness, pairing compatibility, optical contrast, motion tolerance, brand, language, recent-use penalty, and deterministic seed. Creator Taste Memory can re-rank eligible fonts but cannot make an unsafe font eligible.

Composition rules:

- one dominant text idea per frame;
- captions: at most two lines;
- measured with the loaded font before render;
- no face, gesture, CTA, or platform-UI collisions;
- contrast at least 4.5:1, or 3:1 for large display text;
- deliberate hero/support scale contrast;
- no default pills, slabs, glows, gradients, or shadows;
- no all-caps paragraphs, arbitrary highlight, or default serif karaoke;
- frame-driven motion only, with a readable hold and repetition budget.

Typography motion rules:

- captions, editorial statements, proof, utility, and CTA never share one automatic animation policy;
- motion granularity follows meaning: glyph for a short hero word, word for spoken cadence, line/block for editorial composition, stroke for annotation, composite for coordinated hierarchy;
- a text event cannot enter after its spoken idea has passed or exit before the viewer can read it;
- reading duration is estimated from words, characters, language, complexity, role, mobile size, and concurrent imagery—not a universal milliseconds-per-word constant;
- a cut, major camera move, text entrance, visual insert, and SFX do not stack on one instant unless a named climax beat earns the combined load;
- type that is still being read normally survives a cut or the cut is covered by a designed continuation;
- glyph staging uses grapheme clusters and preserves punctuation, numbers, names, URLs, units, ligatures, bidirectional order, and line ownership;
- animation never compensates for weak writing, bad hierarchy, insufficient contrast, or an unsuitable font;
- “show capability” is never a valid semantic reason.

## Pacing, cuts, camera, and transitions

Fast pacing means fast comprehension, not continuous cutting.

Pacing is an inferred sequence property, not BPM, silence count, edit density, or a timer. The planner reasons across an Adaptive Planning Horizon and a whole-short arc: hook, orientation, escalation, proof, release/payoff, and Governed CTA or quiet ending. Each moment declares cognitive load, reading load, visual load, speech pressure, motion energy, novelty, continuity debt, and required recovery.

The shared Sequence Objective and Negative Grammar decide whether a beat needs acceleration, hold, contrast, coverage, or silence. The Short Cadence Adapter then maps that decision to frames. Landscape/Joseph principles remain authoritative; short-form priors only change the acceptable envelopes.

| Treatment | Motivated cuts/min | Reframes/30s | Developments/min |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Founder Podcast | 8–14 | 0–3 | 4–8 |
| Premium Direct Response | 14–22 | 1–4 | 8–14 |
| Minimal Expert | 5–10 | 0–2 | 3–6 |

These are seed priors, never quotas or success metrics. Exceeding them requires Beat Map evidence; staying inside them does not prove good pacing.

A cut is allowed for a new thought/role, verified dead air, completed gesture/reset, proof/contrast reveal, speaker correction, relevant evidence, or a source-safe false-start removal. It is blocked if it clips speech/breath/laugh, breaks semantics, removes a Protected Pause, interrupts a gesture, creates an unmasked pose/room-tone discontinuity, repeats without new cause, or only serves density.

Transcript gaps are not automatically silence. Transcript, VAD, waveform, breath, and rhetoric are reconciled. Low-confidence silence is preserved and surfaced.

Continuous zoom and per-segment resets are prohibited. Normal emphasis is roughly 1.04–1.10x; a major reveal may reach 1.12–1.16x if resolution permits. Moves start before and settle on meaning. Protected Pauses normally hold. Punch-in/out oscillation is blocked. Tracking uses smoothing, dead zones, confidence, velocity, and acceleration limits; uncertain tracking widens or holds rather than chasing.

Hard cuts are default. Dissolves express reflection/time/state. Directional motion follows screen direction. Wipes, overlays, leaks, and match transitions need a named beat cause. Aim for a meaningful development every 3–6 seconds and no more than one major interrupt every 6–9 seconds. Stillness is a valid mode.

Event orchestration uses one shared load budget. Spoken meaning and source continuity rank first, followed by readable type, subject framing, evidence, camera, transition, then SFX decoration. When events compete, lower-priority events move, simplify, or disappear. Repeated beat spacing, uniform alternation, compulsory B-roll, cut-on-every-pause, zoom-on-every-emphasis, and whoosh-on-every-transition are named rhythm failures.

Pacing confidence is reported. Missing transcript, weak prosody, uncertain gesture tracking, VFR drift, or conflicting shot evidence narrows the edit and increases review rather than producing false precision.
### Atomic cut, camera, and transition choreography

A cut event is not proven because a manifest contains `hard` or `zoom_blur`. It is proven only when the renderer changes the source-time mapping or scene source at the intended frame and the accompanying camera/audio behavior is observable. Every combined cut-plus-camera event declares pre-roll, outgoing handle, cut frame, incoming handle, reframe onset, transform origin, target crop, peak velocity, settle frame, hold, recovery, audio continuity, room tone, and fallback. Zoom may anticipate or answer a cut; it may not restart because a new implementation segment began.

The planner evaluates cut alternatives against annotated Joseph and human decisions using tolerance-window precision/recall, pairwise preference, semantic-damage rate, Protected-Pause preservation, pose/gesture continuity, and rhythm-failure rate. Synthetic Joseph heuristics are priors, not ground truth. Hard cuts remain the default; transition overlap duration is included in final composition math so a visual transition cannot desynchronize captions, music, or source mapping.

A cut may use J/L audio, room-tone bridge, B-roll/evidence coverage, designed typography coverage, match motion, or deliberate discontinuity. Each technique needs a cause and handles. When suitable handles or coverage are absent, the system moves the boundary, simplifies, holds, or blocks; it never hides a destructive cut with blur.

## Framing, visuals, and audio

The frame has cooperating regions: subject, editorial typography, evidence, environment, and platform reserve. Only one dominates.

Maintain acceptable face composition for at least 95% of non-B-roll frames; preserve eyes, mouth, chin, headroom, and meaningful hands. Detect underexposure, blur, occlusion, compression, multiple faces, and tracking uncertainty. A bad frame may use relevant coverage, an editorial text scene, or a blocked result; tighter cropping is not always a remedy.

Governed visual modes are Speaker Hero, Editorial Statement, Evidence Insert, Split Proof, Full-Bleed B-roll, Quiet Hold, and Governed CTA. Every asset needs rights, provenance, relevance, and a beat. Decorative filler, irrelevant attractiveness, unsafe generated evidence, and reference pixels are rejected. No suitable asset means source-grounded type or stillness.

Dialogue is the reference bus. Initial targets: dialogue about -16 to -14 LUFS integrated; final true peak below -1 dBTP; music normally 12–20 dB below voiced dialogue context with smooth speech ducking. Measure voiced-region dialogue-to-music ratio, short-term loudness, true peak, and discontinuities—not integrated loudness alone. Ducking declares attack, hold, release, hysteresis, and maximum gain-change rate so breaths and micro-pauses do not pump. Verify mono fold-down, sample rate/channel layout, phone/earbud intelligibility, and consonant/name/number probes in noisy regions. SFX occur only on named beats and never mask consonants, numbers, names, or destinations. If intelligibility is uncertain, reduce/remove music first. The renderer must execute the Audio Plan, not merely carry its ID.
## Portrait compositing, docking, backgrounds, motion graphics, color, and audio execution

The portrait scene is an explicit time-varying layer graph rather than a flat list of overlays:

```text
background plate / environment
  -> behind-subject typography and graphics
  -> subject foreground matte
  -> docked/PiP media and evidence
  -> front captions, utility, CTA, and accessibility overlays
  -> platform reserve visualization in review only
```

Text behind the speaker is a first-class treatment, including when not executed. The planner records desired words, z-order, visible/occluded regions, minimum readable exposure, subject interaction, matte quality requirement, compute tier, and alternate composition. The renderer verifies real occlusion by comparing foreground and text masks over time. Matte edge confidence, temporal consistency, hair/hands/fingers, motion blur, spill, premultiplication, holes, and one-frame dropouts are hard evidence. If the matte is unavailable or unstable, MAUL chooses front/side typography, an editorial text scene, or no candidate and retains the unrealized intent as `accounted-deferred`.

Docking/PiP is a state machine: `full-bleed -> prepare -> enter -> dock -> hold -> handoff -> exit/recover`. Each state owns actual media pixels, geometry, crop, border/chrome, depth, shadow, audio ownership, safe zones, text/evidence slots, and collision behavior. Drawing an empty frame or keeping the speaker full-screen behind decorative chrome is not docking. A missing matte may promote media to a safe full-bleed or split-proof mode only through a recorded fallback.

Background changes are scheduled `BackgroundSceneEvent`s, not palette changes. They can use the source environment, a licensed plate, a source-grounded evidence scene, generated non-evidentiary atmosphere, or an editorial field. The planner governs onset, dwell, transition, depth/parallax, crop, camera relationship, lighting and white-balance match, defocus, contact grounding, source truth, and return continuity. Temporal edge instability, mismatched perspective/light, floating subjects, irrelevant spectacle, and repeated background churn block the event.

Motion graphics run on an optional lane. The planner may request a diagram, annotation, map, interface, counter, proof graphic, or connective accent even when the library contains none. The request survives as a slot with semantic need, timing, layer, anchor/track, brand adaptation, alpha/color space, entrance-hold-exit, retiming limits, collision cost, retrieval/GOD intent, and fallback. Scarcity produces source-grounded typography, a static diagram, Quiet Hold, or `needed-but-unavailable`; it never trains the planner to forget motion graphics exist.

The color pipeline separates technical normalization from creative look. It verifies input primaries, transfer, matrix, range, bit depth, log/gamma, SDR/HDR, and shot consistency before creative treatment. A creative LUT/look requires ID, hash, rights, intended input/output space, strength envelope, per-shot matching, skin-tone protection, highlight/shadow limits, noise and banding checks, scopes, and encoded-output evidence. Unsupported or destructive looks fall back to a safe technical transform; `cinematic grade` cannot be claimed from a CSS filter or warm tint.

The audio renderer executes an event graph, not one looped file. It supports zero or more music tracks/stems with source and rights per item, selected sections, trims, downbeat alignment, BPM/key compatibility where transitions require it, overlaps, crossfades, bridges, bus routing, automation, and treatment-specific development. Two-song edits are permitted when a semantic phase change earns them and the transition is musically and editorially coherent. Dialogue, room tone, music, stems, SFX, and evidence audio have explicit buses and priority. Every planned event must produce an audible native node or a recorded fallback; an `audioPlanId` alone is failure.

## Treatment identity

Founder Podcast earns authority through speaker, editorial support serif, neutral captions, modest motivated camera, proof holds, restrained music, and rare confirmation accents.

Premium Direct Response arranges benefit, friction, exact proof, payoff, and CTA with controlled hero type, faster motivated cuts, relevant evidence, and one hierarchy at a time—without fake urgency, counters, casino effects, or whoosh spam.

Minimal Expert uses precision, negative space, sparse movement, Protected Pauses, one necessary aid, and near-silent texture—without kinetic barrage, decorative B-roll, impact zooms, or empty minimalism.

Treatments must differ in selection, rhythm, hierarchy, camera, type, visuals, and audio—not palette or font size alone.

Treatment identity and motion dialect are related but distinct. A Founder Podcast may use Minimal Founder or Documentary Authority; a Premium Direct Response treatment may use Bold Graphic or a restrained Kinetic Creator dialect. The Treatment Genome records the choice and its reason. The same dialect cannot make candidate treatments duplicates.

Hard failures block export:

- unresolved font, glyph, faux style, or silent hierarchy-changing fallback;

- clipped phoneme, broken sentence, destructive cut, or removed Protected Pause;
- unacceptable crop, tracking chase, identity change, or gesture loss;

- simultaneous cut/camera/type/visual/SFX overload without a named climax beat;
- cosmetic treatment duplication or capability-showcase density without editorial cause;
- CSS/non-frame render animation;

- shared-kernel change that regresses Joseph or landscape behavior without an explicit reviewed migration;
- simulated approval represented as human;
- missing/corrupt/mismatched/unauditable artifact;
- cross-tenant access or contradictory retry/cancel/idempotency result;
- silent intent mutation by memory;
- missing evidence for a quality claim.

- claimed model judgment without an inference receipt or with a provider/model/schema mismatch;
- planner or generator self-certification without an independently calibrated critic and human gate;
- missing required Joseph doctrine row or a Doctrine Traceability Matrix row that ends before observable encoded evidence;
- behind-subject intent silently flattened, unstable matte/occlusion, foreground-edge flicker, or unrecorded compute downgrade;
- decorative docking/PiP chrome without correctly transformed media pixels and state transitions;
- incoherent Genome crossover, undeclared gene borrowing, whole-video template sameness, or duplicate candidates;
- motion-graphics need silently erased because the asset library is sparse;
- unmotivated or temporally unstable background change, perspective/light mismatch, or floating subject integration;

- font choice or pair that bypasses the discovered eligible corpus, compatibility edges, binary/rights filters, or counterfactual graph evidence;
- revision cycle that lowers a gate, changes immutable facts, swaps evaluator authority, oscillates, exceeds budget, or hides an unresolved failure;
- S4/elite label without all mandatory dimension passes, source-suitability disclosure, statistically valid release evidence, and human approval;

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Narrative, cuts, pacing | 25 |
| Writing, typography, captions | 25 |
| Source fidelity | 15 |
| Framing, camera, composition | 15 |
| Dialogue/music/SFX | 10 |

## Edge cases and safe degradation

- No coherent idea: return fewer/none and explain.
- Late hook: source-safe selection without false chronology.
- Low-confidence word: exclude from hero/proof pending review.
- Fast speech: fewer/larger pages, never unreadably small type.
- Long term/URL, acronym, unit, date, currency, percentage, name: preserve and measure semantic grouping.
- Code-switch/non-Latin/RTL/CJK: licensed complete glyph coverage, correct segmentation/order, language-aware line breaking, and review or block.
- Combining mark, ligature, surrogate pair, emoji or zero-width-joiner sequence: preserve the grapheme cluster; approved fallback with provenance or review.
- Missing/corrupt/demo font or missing weight: safe recorded replacement or block; never synthesize.
- No/multiple/occluded face: use speaker evidence, alternate mode, or review; never guess.
- Rapid movement/hand gesture: widen and smooth within limits.
- Dark/noisy/low-resolution source: conservative enhancement, alternate coverage, reduced zoom.
- Vertical/VFR/interlaced/rotated source: normalize and verify A/V mapping first.
- No dead air: preserve cadence; do not manufacture cuts.
- Many pauses or emotional/comedic beat: classify and protect meaning.
- Pose discontinuity: safer boundary, evidence coverage, or designed transition.
- No relevant B-roll: typography or Quiet Hold.
- Attractive irrelevant/unlicensed asset: reject.
- Generated visual resembling evidence: label/redesign/reject.
- Noisy dialogue: local cleanup, lower music, verify intelligibility.
- Conflicting music/SFX: replace, move, attenuate, or remove.
- Provider/render failure: resume deterministically or fail without contradictory export.
- Preview/final mismatch: invalidate approval.
- Container hash mismatch: report decision, decoded-video, decoded-audio, and byte equality separately.
- Partial treatment failure: preserve valid alternatives and lineage.
- Flash/contrast/motion-sensitivity risk: reduce/remove, offer a restrained dialect, and record accessibility evidence.
- Very short phrase: do not stretch into an elaborate program that outlives its meaning.
- Long phrase or rapid speech: fall back from glyph/word staging to readable line/block behavior before shrinking text.
- Multiple words with identical/overlapping timestamps: preserve order, infer only within confidence, and avoid simultaneous karaoke collision.
- Punctuation-only or numeric token: bind to its semantic neighbor unless it is itself the evidenced hero.
- Caption and editorial text duplicate each other: retain the higher-priority role and remove competition.
- Text spans a cut: continue its stable coordinate system, cover the cut, or re-time it; never blink accidentally.
- Primitive dependencies conflict: use compatibility/layer rules or choose a simpler event.
- Selected primitive exceeds render or memory budget: use its declared lower-cost fallback, record deviation, and re-run judgment.
- Preview-only/prototype animation looks attractive: quarantine it until a native frame-driven branch and tests exist.
- Unknown future primitive ID: fail closed; never guess from its name.
- Deprecated primitive in a replay: resolve through a versioned migration or preserve the original renderer version.
- Font loads but metrics differ across renderer/browser: invalidate measurements and block final approval.
- FPS, timebase, or VFR normalization changes: recompile all frame events from source time and verify A/V/text alignment.
- Color or delivery mismatch: verify primaries, transfer, matrix, full/limited range, SDR/HDR tone mapping, rotation, sample aspect ratio, 8/10-bit pixel format, codec/profile, decode integrity, compression, and banding before approval.
- No dialect fits source conditions: use Quiet Hold or the least-invasive source-respectful behavior rather than forcing a branded effect.
- Landscape/Joseph capability does not fit portrait safely: mark it `adaptable`, apply a tested portrait adapter, or choose its governed fallback; never force or silently omit it.
- Reference look requires unavailable cinematography, B-roll, or grade latitude: preserve the trait intent, disclose the limitation, and do not counterfeit evidence.
- Unknown case: preserve source, disclose uncertainty, require review.
- Multimodal planner unavailable or times out: deterministic restrained fallback with downgraded authority, or no candidate; never relabel heuristic output as model judgment.
- Planner receives transcript but no trustworthy visual/temporal context: it may reason about narrative only and cannot claim crop, matte, gesture, camera, background, or visual-composition authority.
- Provider/model version changes or a route silently downgrades: invalidate planner calibration, reference-parity evidence, and affected approval.
- Matte generation exceeds compute cap: retain desired layer intent as `accounted-deferred`, estimate cost, use a reviewed front/side type fallback, and re-run judgment.
- Matte exists but hair, fingers, motion blur, transparency, or fast turns fail continuity: reduce occlusion ambition, switch scene mode, or block.
- Subject occludes too much hero text for its minimum exposure: recompose, time-shift, reveal in front, or abandon the treatment; never call unreadability cinematic depth.
- Docking media lacks resolution or safe crop: use split proof/full bleed or reject rather than enlarging a soft unusable insert.
- Background plate has wrong perspective, lighting, duration, aspect, or parallax latitude: use the source environment/editorial field or no change.
- No motion-graphics asset satisfies a recorded semantic slot: retain `needed-but-unavailable`; use a static/source-grounded aid or Quiet Hold.
- Two music tracks clash in key, BPM, timbre, rights, loudness, or semantic mood: find a bridge, hard-motivated reset, one-track arrangement, or silence.
- LUT expects log input but receives display-referred footage, or HDR metadata is unreliable: bypass the creative LUT, normalize safely, and disclose.
- Genome crossover has no compatible handoff or makes the macro treatment unrecognizable: keep the parent genome, choose another candidate, or block.
- Independent critics disagree or judge confidence is below calibration: require human review; do not average uncertainty into a pass.
- Held-out source is outside V1 Source Scope, including panels, gameplay-first footage, music video, or complex multi-speaker overlap: declare unsupported scope rather than improvising a universal editor.
- Platform UI safe-area profile changes: invalidate affected layout evidence and recompile through the Portrait Format Adapter.

- Whole-source context exceeds planner limits: build hierarchical summaries with evidence links and retain named facts/callbacks; never silently truncate the candidate neighborhood.
- Art Direction Plan conflicts with source truth, brand, accessibility, or available production value: revise the direction or return no candidate rather than costume the source.
- Every beat appears text-worthy: retain dialogue captions as needed, rank editorial text opportunities, preserve visual quiet, and block text-density inflation.
- Compatibility graph has no eligible path for a required language/role pair: use one-family hierarchy or a documented safe fallback, never an unconnected aesthetic guess.
- Revision makes no measurable progress or repeats a prior state: stop, preserve the best valid candidate, require review, or return no candidate.
- S4 target is impossible for the source but a strong restrained edit is possible: label the achieved tier honestly and require the user to choose between restrained export and no S4 candidate.
- Canonical renderer hardware/image/version changes: shadow-render representative fixtures and invalidate S4 evidence until parity is bounded.
- Production source mix or fallback rate drifts outside calibration: disable autonomous approval/S4 labeling, increase review, and trigger rollback or revalidation.
## Review, reliability, cost, and determinism

Preview may be autonomous. Export requires authenticated human action recording reviewer, time, subject, treatment, evidence version, decision, and rationale. Material changes invalidate approval. Simulated approval is test-only and production-blocked.

Replay keys include all quality-relevant artifacts, Capability Ledger version, dialect version, adapter version, font hashes, source normalization, and renderer version. Report decision equality, decoded-video equality, decoded-audio equality, and byte equality separately. Retries safely resume; cancellation prevents late export; partial failures preserve valid artifacts; tenant isolation, deletion, spend caps, quotas, and complete audit are mandatory.

Every native capability has a performance envelope and golden render evidence at representative entry, hold, emphasis, and exit frames. Capability updates invalidate affected evidence. Preview and final must use the same motion calculation and font assets; preview approximations are labeled and cannot receive final approval.

Targets with supplied analysis: first typography/pacing preview under two minutes; warm 30-second final under six minutes; render ratio under 12x; configured cost cap never exceeded. Speed never bypasses a quality gate.
Release state is computed from evidence: `draft`, `planned`, `implemented-unverified`, `native-render-verified`, `encoded-output-verified`, `held-out-reference-parity-passed`, `human-approved`, `blocked`, or `no-approved-candidate`. The UI shows the weakest mandatory capability and every downgrade. No actor can manually type `cinematic` or `complete` into an artifact.

The perceptual critic is calibrated on human-labelled positive and adversarial negative renders, including technically valid but ugly examples. Calibration records model/version, prompt/schema, false-pass and false-fail rates, inter-rater agreement, source-held-out split, treatment-held-out split, and known blind spots. The generator and critic cannot share hidden self-scores as truth. Judge drift or replacement invalidates affected approval evidence.

Production proof uses at least 20-50 diverse held-out V1 clips across speakers, skin tones, lighting, speech rates, languages/glyph sets, source quality, gesture intensity, framing, audio quality, and asset availability. The corpus includes both supplied still traits and multiple licensed/reference-only motion edits with timestamped type, cut, camera, transition, background, matte, color, and sound annotations. One torture fixture may prove wiring; it cannot prove general cinematic judgment.

The release target is measured rather than promised: blind reviewers prefer MAUL over the agreed commodity baseline, identify treatment intent, and judge the export inside the reference quality family. The unacceptable-export false-pass ceiling is below 5% on the held-out set before autonomous release is considered; until then authenticated human approval remains mandatory. Source-limited or dependency-limited runs may increase `no-approved-candidate` frequency rather than lower the quality floor.
## Statistical release proof and production sequencing

The 20-50 clip held-out set is a development and coverage gate, not sufficient by itself to prove an unacceptable-export rate below 5% with strong confidence. Release sample size is calculated from the target rate, desired confidence, stratification, and observed failures. Under the simple zero-failure binomial rule-of-three approximation, at least 60 independent accepted cases are needed before the one-sided 95% upper bound approaches 5%; correlated clips, strata, or any failures require more. The report includes confidence intervals and per-stratum results rather than only a pooled percentage.

Before public launch, production runs in shadow mode, then authenticated internal review, then a bounded canary. Canary policy defines traffic, creator/source strata, minimum observations, hard-stop failures, cost/latency limits, rollback artifact versions, and owner. Drift monitoring tracks source mix, planner/model version, font/asset eligibility, treatment distribution, fallback/deferred/no-candidate rates, critic disagreement, reviewer preference, false-pass discoveries, and renderer/environment changes. A threshold breach disables S4 labeling or rolls back without deleting evidence.

The replay fingerprint pins code and schema versions, planner/critic versions, prompts, font and asset hashes, reference/graph/dialect/template versions, Remotion/Chromium/FFmpeg versions, codec and color configuration, OS/container image, and relevant CPU/GPU execution mode. Hardware-dependent output differences are measured and either bounded or routed to a canonical worker.

Learning is sequential. Creator Taste Memory and Pattern Memory may improve ranking after reviewed production evidence; IRL or learned reward work begins only after the observation/action features and Golden Corpus trajectories are validated. Learning cannot be required to make the first S4 implementation honest, and it cannot silently change a released doctrine or quality threshold.

1. Does the planner receive enough temporal video, composition, gesture, prosody, transcript, source-quality, asset, brand, and reference-trait context to make the decision it claims?

5. Can the system distinguish tasteful stillness from under-editing, energy from chaos, cinematic typography from ornamental text, and source limitation from renderer failure?
6. Do final encoded probes catch deterministic ugliness that unit tests and preview frames miss?
7. Do diverse held-out viewers prefer the work and recognize treatment identity without being shown labels?
